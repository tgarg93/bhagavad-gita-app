## One-page project brief (attach or paste on request)

**Dharma — scholarly content review, Sept 2026**

*What the app is.* Dharma is an iOS learning companion for Hindus in the diaspora — people who grew up around the tradition but were never taught it systematically. It offers a guided journey: an eight-part introduction to Hinduism, a complete narrative Bhagavad Gita (all 18 chapters, 701 verses available), readers for the Ramayana and principal Upanishads, festival guides, deity profiles, a prayer library, and a daily card of bite-sized learning. It launches free on the App Store at Diwali 2026.

*Editorial standards already in place.* Every content claim traces to a named public text; per-section citation footnotes plus item-level source lists; practices without scriptural basis say so honestly; contested or regional practices carry the line "this is one common way — ask your family how they do it"; translations are neutral renderings not tied to one commentarial school.

*What review means.* You receive one Google Doc (one of four content areas, matched to your specialty). Working through it, you flag: (1) factual errors; (2) citations that are wrong, or too weak to carry the claim they support; (3) framings that misrepresent the tradition or flatten significant diversity within it; (4) anything a knowledgeable reader would find embarrassing. You annotate the doc directly; the developer applies fixes and returns disputed points for a second look. You are *not* asked to review prose style, app design, or theology as such.

*Timeline.* Docs distributed Sept 1; annotations due Oct 9; credit sign-off mid-Oct; app live at Diwali (Nov 8).

*Credit.* "Content reviewed by [Name], [degree/affiliation]" in the app's credits screen, on dharma's website, and in the App Store description — exact wording subject to your approval. A reference letter documenting the engagement is available on request.

*Contact.* Tushar Garg · tushargarg93@gmail.com
